# Example 2 — Policy change summary

## Audit context

- Target: an internal operating note issued in March 2026.
- Jurisdiction: not stated in the target.
- Supplied evidence: a 2022 regulator FAQ and a 2025 consultation paper.

## Target text

> The regulator now requires all providers to retain customer verification records for seven years. The new rule took effect on 1 January 2026.

## Supplied evidence

- **2022 FAQ:** providers under the national licensing regime should retain specified verification records for five years.
- **2025 consultation paper:** proposes extending retention to seven years for a subset of high-risk providers; states that final scope and commencement would be determined in a future instrument.

## Expected EvidenceGuard reasoning

The combined claim is `unsupported`.

- Report `citation_role_mismatch` because a consultation proposal is not a binding final requirement.
- Report `partial_support` only if the audit separates the historical five-year requirement from the asserted seven-year rule; the sources do not support “all providers.”
- Report `stale_or_insufficient_source` because the 2022 FAQ and consultation paper cannot establish a rule allegedly effective in 2026. The problem is fitness and authority, not age alone.
- Report `unclear_population_or_timeframe` because the target does not identify the jurisdiction or covered provider class, and the effective date is unsupported.

## Remediation

Obtain the final controlling instrument and confirm its jurisdiction, covered entities, record classes, version, and commencement date. Until then, do not communicate the seven-year statement as a current requirement.

## Escalation

Escalate to the policy or legal owner because the controlling authority is absent and the note may direct operational compliance.
